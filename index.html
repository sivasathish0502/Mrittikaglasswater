We drink water.
But what holds it matters.

Plastic containers are convenient, but widespread reliance on disposable and single-use plastic creates a growing waste challenge.

  • Plastic waste accumulation
  • Dependence on disposable packaging
  • Environmental burden from production and disposal
  • Potential chemical migration under certain conditions
  • Difficulty creating a truly reusable, premium drinking experience

Not every plastic container is unsafe. The concern is the disposable habit — and what it leaves behind.

Conventional plasticReusable glass

More things worth knowing

Microorganisms & biofilm

Scratches and wear on reused plastic can trap residue, and a thin layer of microorganisms (biofilm) may build up where cleaning can’t reach.

With glassA smooth, hard surface is easier to clean and sanitize thoroughly between every fill.

Microplastic particles

Studies have found tiny plastic particles in bottled water, and wear from reuse, cap twisting and handling can add more. Health effects are still being studied.

With glassGlass doesn’t shed plastic particles into the water.

Heat & chemical migration

Heat and sunlight during storage and transport can increase the chance of chemicals moving from some plastics into water.

With glassGlass is chemically inert, so the water stays as it was filled.

Odour, taste & waste

Plastic can pick up odours and flavours, and ageing containers turn cloudy. They’re discarded, and most never get recycled.

With glassClear, neutral and built for many refills, so the vessel stays in use instead of becoming waste.
